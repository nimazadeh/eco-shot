## Consumer protection in distance sales (Electronic Commerce Law)

All of this is from L1 [T] unless stated.
- **Article 33.** Before the contract, in good time, the seller must give the buyer information that affects the decision, at least: (a) technical specifications and functional features; (b) the supplier's identity, trade name and address; (c) an email address, phone number or other way to contact the seller; (d) every cost the buyer will bear (price, tax, shipping, call cost); (e) how long the offer is valid; (f) the contract terms and process, including payment, delivery or performance, cancellation, referral and after-sales service.
- **Article 34.** Separately, while confirming that preliminary information, the supplier must send: the business address for complaints; warranty and after-sales information; the conditions and process of withdrawal under articles 37 and 38; the cancellation conditions of service contracts.
- **Article 35.** This information and its confirmation must be on a durable medium, clear and explicit, delivered in good time.
- **Article 37.** In any distance transaction the consumer has at least **seven working days** to withdraw without penalty or giving a reason. The only cost the consumer bears is returning the goods.
- **Article 38.** The period starts, for goods, on delivery and, for services, on the day of the contract, and in every case only after the supplier has given the information of articles 33 and 34. On withdrawal the supplier must refund the exact amount received as soon as possible, asking for no money. The right does not apply where special conditions govern the goods or services, listed in a regulation.
- **Article 39.** If, at the time of the deal, the supplier cannot perform because the goods are out of stock or the service cannot be performed, it must **refund immediately**, except where performance is not permanently impossible and the buyer is ready to wait. If the supplier knew from the start it could not perform, it must refund and gets the maximum penalty under the law.
- **Articles 40 to 43.** A supplier may deliver similar goods only if it announced that before or during the deal. Wrong goods go back at the supplier's cost. The chapter does not apply to financial services (per regulation), real estate sales (not leases), vending machines, public phones, and auctions. Silence is not consent.
- **Article 46.** Contract terms that contradict this chapter, and unfair terms against the consumer, have no effect.

**Exceptions to the withdrawal right (L2 [T]).** Unless the parties agree otherwise, there is no right of withdrawal for: (a) services whose provision began, by agreement with the consumer, before the seven working days ended; (b) food and other goods for daily consumption; (c) goods or services priced by financial-market swings outside the supplier's control; (d) goods made to the consumer's individual specification, clearly personal, or that by nature cannot be returned or spoil quickly; (e) **packaged audio or video tapes and packaged computer software that the consumer has opened**; (f) newspapers and magazines. Financial services excluded from the chapter are investment, insurance and other financial and credit institutions' services.

**Digital codes and downloadable files are not named.** Items (d) and (e) are the closest; (e) speaks of packaged software that has been opened. Whether an instantly delivered code or a downloaded file counts is a question for a lawyer. A secondary article asserts that downloadable software is excluded once delivered or activated [S]; that is an interpretation, not the text.

**Torob's terms (T1 [P]).** Torob requires its shops to follow the e-commerce law, says the law outranks the shop's own rules, and lists duties that mirror articles 33 to 41 (state shipping terms, time and cost before ordering, send an order number and invoice, answer complaints, ship the right item).

## VAT

- The VAT law of 1400, article 7, sets the standard rate at 9 percent [S]. A tax-administration notice reproduced by software vendors says the 1403 budget law raised it by one point, from 9 to 10 percent [S]. A budget-bill report says the total rate stays 10 percent in 1404 [S].
- **The 1405 rate is not confirmed from an official source.** Secondary pages claim online shops must register for VAT [S].
- Whether shown prices must include VAT is a business rule (money rules ticket); Torob wants one all-in price per option (T1 [P]).

## Electronic invoices (Moodian system)

Only secondary sources were reachable [S, X]. They claim that: the duty to issue and register electronic invoices depends on annual sales thresholds that step down in phases (one article states a 1405 threshold of 12 billion Toman); invoices must be registered within 12 days; the penalty for missing invoices is 10 percent of the unreported sales or at least 20 million Rials, whichever is higher; and people outside the obliged categories may not charge VAT to buyers. None of this was checked against the tax authority or the law text.

## Enamad (electronic trust symbol)

- Torob and Emalls both require it for online shops (T1 [P]).
- Secondary guides say: natural persons need a national ID card and birth certificate, a mobile number in their own name, a domain registered in their name and contact details; legal persons need company registration documents; the Enamad system was merged with the "Emta" e-commerce customer-authentication system; a gateway wants the documents, bank account and Enamad in one person's name [S]. Not confirmed on `enamad.ir`.

## Legal pages

No official list of "pages a shop must show" was found. Articles 33 to 35 imply, before the contract: seller identity, address and contact, full costs, offer validity, payment, delivery, cancellation and after-sales terms, and the withdrawal conditions on a durable medium. Torob's terms add: shop name rules and shipping and price clarity.

## Questions for the owner's accountant or lawyer

1. Is the business a VAT payer? What is the 1405 rate, and must shown prices include it?
2. Which Moodian obligations apply at our expected turnover? Who registers, which invoice type, which deadlines? Do online shops register whatever their turnover?
3. Do instantly delivered **digital codes** and **downloadable files** fall under the withdrawal exceptions? How should the refund policy for them be worded?
4. What exactly must the pre-contract information (article 33) and the separate confirmation (article 34) contain, and is an SMS plus the order page a valid durable medium?
5. Whose name must the domain, Enamad, bank account and tax file carry?
6. How long must invoices and orders be kept, and what privacy duties apply to stored customer phone numbers and addresses?

## What this means for later tickets (facts to weigh, not decisions)

- **Orders and after-sales.** Seven working days to withdraw for physical goods from the delivery date; a refund of the exact amount, with the buyer paying only the return shipping; if goods are out of stock after payment, refund immediately unless the buyer agrees to wait.
- **Digital delivery.** The code-stock-runs-out case is governed by article 39 (immediate refund unless the buyer waits). Withdrawal for delivered digital goods is a legal grey area (question 3).
- **Checkout.** Before ordering the buyer must see all costs, the seller's identity, address and contact, the offer's validity and the cancellation and after-sales terms; a separate confirmation follows the order.
- **Money rules.** VAT display and rate are open questions 1 and 2.
- **Business accounts.** The Enamad and its owner details come first.
